About

Overview

I currently coordinate digital pathology at NYU Langone Health's Tisch Hospital, where I support the anatomic pathology team's move to whole-slide imaging. Before that I spent five years in the Molecular Diagnostics Laboratory of the Wildlife Conservation Society at the Bronx Zoo, first as a technician and then in the pathology lab, working across the Zoological Health Program's diagnostic caseload.

My work sits where assay development meets field conservation: multiplex qPCR panels for the illegal wildlife trade, pathogen screening for chelonians and iguanas from Belize to Madagascar, novel herpesvirus and papillomavirus characterization in zoo collections, and environmental DNA surveys ranging from the New York Bight to the southern flank of Mount Everest. I am the author or co-author of four peer-reviewed papers and five conference abstracts.

I am a part-time master's student in bioinformatics at NYU Tandon School of Engineering, taking the degree alongside the hospital job. Before that I earned a B.A. in Environmental Studies from Purchase College, SUNY, where my thesis used MinION nanopore sequencing to trace mercury through songbird diets in Acadia National Park.

Canine distemper virus — phylodynamics of a wildlife-maintained lineage

Core analysis complete · 2025 – 2026 · MIT licensed

Canine distemper virus (Morbillivirus canis) circulates in North American carnivores, but it is not obvious whether a given wildlife lineage sustains itself or is reseeded from domestic dogs. This project tests that directly: 162 public hemagglutinin sequences collected between 1992 and 2023, 95% of them from wild hosts — mostly raccoon, with coyote, striped skunk and red fox — assigned to lineage America-2 and analysed under a Bayesian phylodynamic model with discrete host-state reconstruction.

The host transition rates separate cleanly in one direction. Raccoon-to-wild-canid transmission is credibly elevated and raccoon-to-domestic-dog transmission credibly depressed, and ten of thirteen well-supported host transitions on the tree begin in procyonids. The lineage is being maintained in wildlife, not topped up from dogs. As a blind check on the reconstruction, it independently recovered two captive felid clusters twenty-three years apart — a lion and a leopard around 1992, and four tigers at an Indiana rescue centre around 2015 — each matching a separately published outbreak investigation, from sequence data and host labels alone.

Not claimed: the root state is unresolved and no inference is made about where the lineage originated. The trait model is symmetric, the locus is single, and sampling reflects surveillance effort rather than prevalence. Limitations are documented in full in the repository's methods.

Inference
BEAST 2.7.7 — two chains of 108 states, HKY+Γ4, uncorrelated relaxed lognormal clock, constant-size coalescent; all ESS above 500
Pipeline
Nine numbered Python scripts from GenBank retrieval through metadata curation, MAFFT alignment and IQ-TREE ML phylogeny, alignment QC, clade delineation, stratified subsampling and BEAST2 XML generation; every excluded sequence logged with a machine-readable reason
Viewer
Nextstrain community build, served in Auspice with H-domain annotation in A75/17 coordinates
Reproducibility
Pinned conda environment, deterministic subsampling, cached accession list; outputs regenerate from the accession list rather than being committed

ggbrn

In development · 2026 · not yet public

An R package that settles my ggplot2 defaults once instead of re-deriving them in every analysis script. It ships a theme, a set of discrete and continuous scales, and the palettes used across this site, so a figure drafted in a notebook and a figure dropped into a manuscript or a talk come out of the same place.

The palettes are the ramp from the mark at the top of this page — a cool-to-warm eight-stop sequence with a matched dark-ground variant — which makes it usable for ordered categories and continuous fills, not only for decoration.

Provides
theme_brn(), discrete and continuous scale constructors, and the palette set in both light and dark variants
Built on
ggplot2, following its extension conventions so the scales compose with the rest of the grammar rather than overriding it
Reproducibility
Version-pinned under renv, so a figure regenerates identically across machines
